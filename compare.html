What an HRMS actually needs to do (in India, in 2026)

An HRMS — Human Resources Management System — is the operating system for your people. It should hold your employee records, run your payroll, track attendance and leave, manage recruitment, handle expenses and timesheets, and close out exits cleanly. In India specifically, it has one extra job most overseas tools don't think about: keep you compliant with statutory filings. PF, ESI, Professional Tax, TDS, Form 16 — all of it should happen as a side-effect of running payroll, not as a quarterly emergency.

That's the bar. Most HRMS tools clear part of it. Few clear all of it without selling you three add-ons.

The 11 things to evaluate

  1. Payroll with Indian statutory engine — PF, ESI, PT (state-wise), TDS, gratuity, Form 16. If a tool sells "payroll" as an add-on, it's not really an HRMS for India.
  2. Attendance & leave — check-in/out, leave balances, carry-forward rules, team calendars. Should work on mobile, not just desktop.
  3. Recruitment with a public careers page — a hosted URL where candidates apply, with a pipeline behind it. Otherwise you're back to email threads.
  4. Expenses — claims, receipt uploads, approval workflow. Bonus for OCR.
  5. Timesheets — weekly entry, project allocation, manager approval. Critical for services companies.
  6. Exit & clearance — resignation flow, clearance from each department, full and final settlement. The module everyone forgets to ask about until they need it.
  7. Policies — version-controlled, with mandatory acknowledgements and an audit trail. Auditors love this.
  8. Reports & analytics — headcount, attrition, leave patterns, payroll trends, exportable to CSV.
  9. Roles & permissions — granular per-role toggles. A finance manager shouldn't see leave balances; a team lead shouldn't see salaries.
  10. Multi-tenant security — row-level security so your data is genuinely isolated from other customers'.
  11. Honest pricing — published per-employee per-month rates, not "contact sales for a quote".

How to evaluate in two weeks, not two quarters

  1. Week 1, day 1–2: Sign up for free trials of two or three tools. No sales calls.
  2. Day 3: Add five real employees. Try a leave application. See how many clicks it takes.
  3. Day 4: Run a sample payroll for one of those employees. Check if PF, ESI and TDS are calculated correctly.
  4. Day 5: Post a fake job. See how the public careers page looks.
  5. Week 2: Have one HR person and one engineer use the tool for a full week. Decide based on how it felt, not what the demo showed.
